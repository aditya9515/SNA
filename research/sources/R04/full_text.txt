GATFELPA integrates graph 
attention networks and enhanced 
label propagation for robust 
community detection
Feiyi Tang1, Junxian Li1,2, Xi Liu1,2, Chao Chang1,2 & Luyao Teng1
Community detection in graph networks is a fundamental yet challenging task due to limitations in 
existing methods. Traditional Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)–based models often suffer from 
over-smoothing, which results in indistinguishable node representations after excessive aggregation. 
Additionally, many models face computational inefficiency, restricting their scalability on large-scale 
networks. To address these challenges, we propose GATFELPA, a hybrid community detection model 
that combines a Graph Attention Network (GAT) with an enhanced label propagation algorithm 
(DPCELPA). GATFELPA employs an adaptive strategy to dynamically determine the optimal number of 
aggregation layers, effectively mitigating over-smoothing by balancing intra-community compactness 
and inter-community separability. A novel similarity preservation module further enhances the model’s 
ability to differentiate communities by retaining local and global dissimilarities in heterogeneous 
networks. Comparative experiments on four real-world datasets, including large-scale networks 
like ogbn-arxiv, demonstrate that GATFELPA achieves superior performance across most metrics, 
particularly excelling in accuracy, robustness, and scalability. These results highlight GATFELPA as a 
promising approach for addressing complex community detection tasks.
Keywords Community detection, Graph attention network (GAT), Density peak clustering, Label 
propagation algorithm (LPA)
With the rapid advancement of deep learning technologies, many researchers have explored graph clustering 
methods attributed to community detection 1. Although these methods effectively classify nodes within 
attributed graphs, they often overlook the structural properties of communities, particularly the principle of 
” dense connections within communities and sparse connections between them”2.
Recent methods based on Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have achieved notable performance 
improvements, but still face inherent challenges 3. A significant limitation is the increased computational load 
caused by complex convolution operations and nonlinear activation functions, particularly on large datasets. 
Moreover, shallow GCNs struggle to capture long-range dependencies due to their limited propagation range, 
while deep GCNs risk over-smoothing, which makes node representations indistinguishable. The scarcity of 
labeled data in real-world networks further exacerbates these issues, as GCNs often require extensive labeled 
data for effective training4.
To address these challenges, various solutions have been proposed. Wu et al. introduced the Simplified Graph 
Convolution Network (SGC) to eliminate resource-intensive non-linearity between GCN layers 5. However, 
SGC inherits GCN’s inability to emphasize the importance of certain neighboring nodes. Zhang et al. utilized 
community distance metrics to determine aggregation layers without additional parameters, but their method 
overlooks the importance of inter-community distances for smoothness evaluation 6. Meanwhile, Chen et al. 
proposed MAD and MADGap to measure over-smoothness but introduced new parameters in the process7.
Both Label Propagation Algorithms (LPA) and GCN-based methods are message-passing algorithms that 
can solve community detection tasks. While LPA propagates node labels through the edges, GCNs propagate and 
transform node features8. Efforts to integrate these two approaches have shown potential to improve community 
detection, but require further optimization.
In response to these challenges, this paper proposes GATFELPA, a novel hybrid model that integrates the 
DPCELPA9 algorithm, previously introduced in our work, with a linear Graph Attention Network (GAT).

GATFELPA is composed of three core components: a GAT module, a similarity preservation module, and the 
enhanced label propagation mechanism, DPCELPA. Although many existing community detection models, 
such as DDGAE, achieve high accuracy, their computational intensity limits the scalability on large-scale 
networks. This paper addresses these limitations by focusing on the computational efficiency of GATFELPA, 
with a detailed complexity analysis and comparisons against baseline models such as GAE, VGAE, DDGAE, and 
GCN-based models. The findings highlight the superior trade-off between computational cost and performance, 
particularly for large-scale datasets.
In the GAT module, the Jaccard index is incorporated creatively into the attention mechanism, eliminating 
the need to learn additional attention parameters while implicitly capturing the local structural relationships 
within the graph4. Furthermore, the linear GAT, based on SGC, is employed to differentiate nodes of varying 
importance effectively. To enhance model adaptability, DPCELPA is utilized to dynamically determine the 
optimal number of aggregation layers by evaluating intra-community and inter-community distances, ensuring 
the model achieves optimal aggregation while mitigating over-smoothing. Complementing this, the similarity 
preservation module ensures that the smoothed features retain local similarity while maintaining global 
dissimilarity, capturing the structural distinctions necessary for effective community detection. The final step 
applies DPCELPA to the smoothed features, yielding highly accurate and stable community assignments.
To validate the proposed model, this study conducts comprehensive experiments on four benchmark datasets: 
Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, and ogbn-arxiv, alongside 20 other algorithms. The experimental results demonstrate 
that GATFELPA consistently outperforms competing methods in terms of accuracy, Normalized Mutual 
Information (NMI), Adjusted Rand Index (ARI), and F1-score across various network sizes and complexities. 
Ablation studies confirm the importance of each module, parameter analyses underline the model’s robustness 
across diverse scenarios, and computational complexity comparison demonstrates the efficiency of GATFELPA ’s 
hybrid mechanism.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• Fusion of GAT and DPCELP A: The GATFELPA model integrates the attention mechanism of GAT with the 
label propagation strategy of DPCELPA, leveraging both local node similarities and global network structures 
for effective community detection.
• Adaptive Aggregation Strategy: An adaptive strategy is introduced to dynamically determine the optimal 
number of aggregation layers by evaluating intra-community and inter-community distances. This reduces 
over-smoothing while improving clustering performance and computational efficiency.
• Similarity Preservation Module: This module ensures that smoothed features retain local similarity while 
maintaining global distinctiveness, enabling the model to capture essential structural characteristics critical 
for accurate and robust community detection.
• Extensive Experimental Validation:  GATFELPA is evaluated on four real-world datasets-Cora, CiteSeer, 
PubMed, and ogbn-arxiv-through comparative experiments, ablation studies, parameter analyses, and com-
putational complexity comparison. The results consistently demonstrate that GATFELPA achieves superior 
performance across diverse metrics and datasets.
• Computational Efficiency:  A thorough computational complexity analysis reveals that GATFELPA offers 
a favorable balance between accuracy and scalability, making it particularly suitable for large-scale datasets, 
where traditional models like DDGAE struggle.The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 
2 reviews related work, summarizing traditional methods and recent advancements in community detection. 
Section 3 details the GATFELPA model, describing its components and algorithmic framework. Section 4

Related work
Community detection has been a focal point of research, with numerous approaches proposed over the years. 
Traditional methods include modularity-based algorithms, such as those introduced by Newman and Girvan 
in 2004 10, which optimize modularity scores to identify community structures. Similarly, spectral clustering 
methods decompose the graph’s adjacency matrix to uncover latent community structures11. While foundational, 
these methods often face limitations in scalability and the integration of node attributes, which are critical for 
modern network analysis.
Label Propagation Algorithms (LPAs)8 offer a computationally efficient alternative by iteratively propagating 
node labels through the graph edges. LPAs are particularly suitable for large-scale networks due to their simplicity 
and scalability. However, their reliance on random initialization of labels and non-deterministic update orders 
often leads to instability and reduced accuracy. To address these limitations, Adaptive Label Propagation 
(ALP)12 introduces a mechanism to assign adaptive weights to neighbors, which enhances the detection of 
overlapping communities in heterogeneous networks. Although ALP demonstrates improved performance in 
specific scenarios, it incurs higher computational costs and relies on carefully tuned parameters, which limit its 
generalizability across diverse datasets.
The advent of deep learning has transformed community detection, particularly with the development of 
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). GCNs aggregate features from neighboring nodes through convolution 
operations, integrating both network topology and node attributes13. Despite their success, GCNs face challenges 
such as over-smoothing, where excessive aggregation renders node features indistinguishable. To address 
this, Wu et al. proposed Simplified Graph Convolutional Networks (SGCs) 5, which eliminate non-linearities 
to streamline the model while retaining performance. Building on the limitations of GCNs, GraphSAGE 14 
introduces a sampling-based aggregation mechanism, which selects a fixed number of neighbors for feature

nodes, making it particularly suitable for dynamic networks. However, its reliance on sampling can lead to the 
omission of important structural information in certain cases.
In parallel, Velickovic et al. introduced Graph Attention Networks (GATs)15, which assign varying importance 
to neighboring nodes through attention mechanisms, enhancing the model’s ability to capture salient features. 
Building on this foundation, Overlapping Community Detection using GATs (AOCD) 16 integrates attention 
mechanisms into the Neural Overlapping Community Detection (NOCD) framework, achieving notable 
improvements in overlapping community detection. However, AOCD is limited in its robustness when applied 
to non-overlapping or hybrid approaches that combine attention mechanisms with label propagation. GATv217 
further refines the attention mechanism by introducing a dynamic attention weight computation, which models 
the relationships between nodes as a learnable function of edge features. This approach improves the ability to 
capture asymmetric relationships but introduces additional computational overhead in large-scale networks.
Efforts to combine GCNs with LPAs have shown promise. Approximate Personalized Propagation of 
Neural Predictions (APPNP)18 separates neural network predictions from information propagation, leveraging 
Personalized PageRank to enhance label propagation. This method effectively mitigates over-smoothing by 
balancing the influence of local and global neighbors through a damping factor. Expanding on this, Semi-
Supervised Label Propagation 19 incorporates labeled data to guide community detection within a GNN 
framework, outperforming unsupervised methods. However, its reliance on labeled data restricts its applicability 
in scenarios with limited annotations.
The challenge of over-smoothing has also driven the development of advanced architectures capable of deeper 
representations. GCNII20 addresses this problem by incorporating identity mapping and residual connections 
to preserve feature distinctiveness across layers. It also introduces layer-wise weight adjustment, which balances 
feature reuse and new information aggregation, allowing the model to achieve high performance in complex 
graphs. Despite these advancements, GCNII’s fixed-depth design may limit its adaptability to networks with 
diverse structural properties.
Recent advancements have addressed these challenges through novel architectures. For example, Dwivedi 
and Bresson generalized transformer networks to graph data, demonstrating improved performance in capturing 
complex node dependencies 21. Liu et al. categorized deep learning approaches for community detection 
into three streams: deep neural networks, deep graph embedding, and graph neural networks, identifying 
key challenges and future research directions 22. Furthermore, Su et al. provided a comprehensive survey on 
community detection using deep learning, evaluating the effectiveness of various models in handling complex 
network structures 23. Beyond Homophily 24 reconstructs graph structures into homophilic and heterophilic 
components, enabling dual clustering networks to extract both low- and high-frequency information. While 
effective for graphs with diverse homophily levels, this approach introduces significant computational overhead 
due to explicit graph reconstruction. Similarly, CDC 25 employs adaptive anchor selection and graph filtering, 
achieving state-of-the-art scalability in large datasets, such as Papers100M. However, the CDC focuses primarily 
on general graph clustering and lacks mechanisms to balance global structural learning with local feature 
preservation, which is critical for precise community detection.
Our prior work introduced DPC-LPA9, which combines the clustering of density peaks with the propagation 
of labels. By identifying community centers through improved density peak clustering and refinement of 
label propagation based on node similarity and weight, DPC-LPA addresses the instability and inefficiency of 
traditional LPAs. It demonstrated superior performance in modularity and stability compared to methods like 
DCN, WCF-LPA, and CLPE, across various real-world datasets. Building on these advancements, this paper 
proposes GATFELPA, which integrates the attention mechanism of GAT with the DPC-LPA label propagation 
strategy. The attention mechanism enhances the model’s ability to focus on relevant node features, while DPC-
LPA provides a robust framework for label propagation. Additionally, including a similarity preservation 
module ensures that smoothed features retain local similarity and global distinctiveness. This hybrid design 
enables accurate and scalable community detection, particularly for large-scale, sparse networks with rich node 
attributes.
Proposed algorithm
To tackle the indiscriminate aggregation of neighboring nodes and the over-smoothing issue in traditional GCN 
models, we propose the GATFELPA model, which combines DPCELPA with Graph Attention Networks (GAT). 
The model uses the Jaccard index in the GAT’s attention mechanism to accurately gauge the importance of 
neighboring nodes, thus avoiding indiscriminate aggregation. An adaptive strategy based on intra-community 
and inter-community distances is introduced to evaluate the smoothness of node representations and determine 
the optimal number of aggregation layers. Additionally, a similarity-preserving module ensures that the 
smoothed features exhibit both local similarity and global dissimilarity. The final community detection results 
are obtained using DPCELPA on the smoothed features. The framework of the GATFELPA model is illustrated 
in Fig. 1.
As shown in Fig. 1, the GATFELPA framework consists of three key stages. First, the Graph Attention Network 
(GAT) aggregates features while weighting the importance of neighbors. Second, the Similarity Preservation 
Module balances local and global node relationships. Finally, the DPCELPA algorithm generates community 
labels based on the refined node embeddings.

Adaptive calculation of aggregation layers
In GCN-based models, determining the optimal number of aggregation layers k is pivotal to achieving smooth 
and meaningful node features. Shallow GCNs have limited propagation ranges, restricting their ability to 
capture global structures. Conversely, deep GCNs risk over-smoothing, where node representations become 
indistinguishable, undermining their capacity to differentiate communities. To address this, the GATFELPA 
model incorporates an adaptive strategy that dynamically determines k based on intra-community and inter-
community structural metrics.

community connections. To quantify these properties, two metrics are introduced:
• Intra-community distance (intra (C)): Measures the density of connections within communities, reflecting 
internal cohesiveness.

to the structural characteristics of the graph. This approach not only mitigates over-smoothing but also aligns 
the aggregation process with the principle of ” dense intra-community connections and sparse inter-community 
connections. ” The result is a robust and adaptive framework that preserves the integrity of community structures 
while improving the performance of the detection task.
Similarity preservation
To enhance the accuracy of community detection, we introduce a similarity preservation module that emphasizes 
the retention of local similarity while ensuring global dissimilarity among nodes. This module aims to align the 
community division with both the network’s topology and its attribute information, preserving the structural 
integrity of the graph.
In the similarity preservation module, the Jaccard coefficient is used to quantify the similarity between each 
node and all other nodes. Based on this measure:
• The top sim_num most similar nodes are selected for each node to form the set of similar node pairs Ss, 
representing strong intra-community connections.
• The top dissim_num most dissimilar nodes are identified to construct the set of dissimilar node pairs Sd, 
reflecting inter-community sparsity.Each node pair is assigned a binary label:

smaller subsets iteratively, thus improving computational efficiency and comprehensively extracting informa-

Experimental analysis
To evaluate the performance and verify the effectiveness of the GATFELPA model, we conduct a series of 
comparative experiments, ablation studies, parameter sensitivity analyses, and computational complexity 
comparisons using four real-world network datasets. These experiments aim to assess the model’s accuracy, 
robustness, scalability, and computational efficiency across varying network structures and sizes.
Datasets
The experiments utilized four widely studied real-world datasets: Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, and ogbn-arxiv. 
Table 2 summarizes the key characteristics of these datasets. The first three datasets are benchmark citation 
networks commonly used in graph-based community detection studies. Each dataset represents a network 
where nodes correspond to publications, edges denote citation relationships, and features describe attributes 
extracted from text. These datasets vary in size and complexity, enabling a thorough evaluation of GATFELPA ’s 
performance across different scales and structures.
Additionally, we evaluated GATFELPA on the ogbn-arxiv dataset, a large-scale citation network containing 
169,343 nodes and 1,166,243 edges, classified into 40 categories. This dataset serves as a challenging benchmark 
for assessing the model’s scalability and efficiency when applied to extensive networks with high-dimensional 
features and complex topologies.

encoder network to iteratively refine feature representations and employs a self-supervised mechanism to

• DDGAE38: A Deep Attentional Graph Autoencoder that fuses structural, attribute, and modular informa-

anism.
• Beyond Homophily24: A method that reconstructs graphs by incorporating both homophilic and het -

deviating from traditional homophilic assumptions.
• CDC25: A contrastive learning-based framework designed to handle complex datasets. CDC learns embed-
dings that integrate structural and attribute information, achieving robust performance on clustering tasks.
• AOCD16: A method that enhances the NOCD framework by incorporating GATs. AOCD is particularly 
effective in identifying overlapping communities by leveraging attention mechanisms to assign varying 
importance to neighboring nodes, but it lacks optimization for non-overlapping or hybrid community 
detection.
• Semi-Supervised Label Propagation 19: A semi-supervised approach that combines GNNs with label 
propagation, using labeled data to guide clustering. While it achieves superior results compared to tra -
ditional unsupervised methods, its dependency on labeled data limits its applicability in large, unlabeled 
networks.

Parameter settings
The GATFELPA model employs an adaptive strategy to dynamically determine the number of aggregation 
layers k, optimizing the smoothness of node representations as described in Sect. 3.2. This adaptive approach is 
rigorously evaluated in Sect.4.3 to highlight its impact on the model’s performance. The parameters used for the 
GATFELPA model across all datasets are set as follows:
• Learning Rate: Fixed at 0.001 to ensure stable convergence during training.
• sim_num: Set to 0.01| N|, representing 1% of the total number of nodes, to select the most similar node 
pairs.
• Dropout Rate: Fixed at 0.8 to prevent overfitting and enhance model generalization.
• Single-layer Neural Network Size:  Set to 500, providing sufficient capacity to capture meaningful feature 
representations.Given the imbalance between the number of similar and dissimilar node pairs, sim_num 
is varied to assess the robustness of the model. Specifically, dissim_num is set to 0.6|N|, 0.7|N|, 0.8|N|, and 
0.9|N|, representing 60%, 70%, 80%, and 90% of the total number of nodes, respectively. This variation allows 
a thorough exploration of the model’s sensitivity to the relative proportions of similar and dissimilar pairs.
These parameter settings are chosen to balance computational efficiency and clustering accuracy, ensuring the 
model performs well across datasets of varying scales and complexities. By systematically varying dissim_num
, the model’ s adaptability and robustness in handling diverse community structures are further validated. The 
fixed learning rate and dropout rate align with established practices in graph neural networks, providing a stable 
foundation for reproducibility and comparative analysis.
This detailed parameter configuration ensures that the GATFELPA model is evaluated under consistent 
and rigorous conditions, facilitating a fair comparison with baseline methods while emphasizing the model’ s 
flexibility and scalability.

The impact of k value on experimental results
This section examines how the number of aggregation layers, k, influences the performance of the GATFELPA 
model. Using the adaptive strategy outlined in Sect.3.2, the optimal k values for the Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, and 
ogbn-arxiv datasets are determined to be 8, 6, 25, and 25, respectively.
The results in Fig.  2 highlight the effectiveness of the proposed strategy, which evaluates both inter-
community ( inter_intra) and intra-community ( d_intra) distances to balance community separability 
and compactness. On the PubMed and ogbn-arxiv datasets, d_intra remains negative for small k, reflecting 
compact intra-community structures. However, when k> 45, d_intra trends toward zero, indicating reduced 
compactness without sufficient improvement in separability. The proposed strategy identifies k = 25 as optimal, 
achieving a balance where inter_intra maximizes separability and d_intra retains compactness.
This trend is consistent across datasets. The adaptive strategy prevents over-smoothing of node representations 
by selecting appropriate k values, which is crucial for optimizing community detection. Notably, the strategy 
enhances the robustness and scalability of GATFELPA, especially on large datasets like PubMed and ogbn-arxiv.
Figure 3 and Table 4 further validate the effectiveness of the strategy by examining its impact on evaluation 
metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, and F1). The metrics generally increase with k initially but degrade when k becomes 
too large, indicating over-smoothing. GATFELPA achieves near-optimal results with the k values determined by 
the adaptive strategy, demonstrating its reliability in balancing performance and robustness.
To further validate the proposed method, we compared it with a fixed k and considered only d_intra 
without incorporating inter_intra. On the PubMed dataset, the results reveal that relying solely on d_intra 
fails to identify the optimal k. The fixed approach often overemphasizes compactness at the cost of separability, 
leading to suboptimal clustering performance. In contrast, the proposed adaptive strategy effectively balances 
intra-community compactness and inter-community separability, resulting in robust and scalable performance 
across all datasets.
In conclusion, the adaptive strategy effectively balances community separability and compactness, enabling 
consistent and robust performance across diverse datasets. This demonstrates the efficacy of the proposed 
method in optimizing community detection with GATFELPA.

Conclusions
This paper addresses the limitations of traditional Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and their overlap 
with Label Propagation Algorithms (LPA) by proposing GATFELPA, a novel community detection model for 
graph networks. GATFELPA integrates the strengths of attention mechanisms, similarity preservation, and 
adaptive aggregation to achieve superior performance in community detection tasks.
The model innovatively employs the Jaccard coefficient in its attention mechanism, enabling efficient 
integration of local graph information without introducing additional parameters. This design preserves the 
computational simplicity of Simplified Graph Convolution (SGC) while overcoming its inability to distinguish 
the varying importance of neighboring nodes. As a result, GATFELPA effectively captures both structural and 
attribute-based information in graphs.
To address the challenges of over-smoothing and limited propagation ranges in GCNs, GATFELPA adopts an 
adaptive strategy, embedded within the DPCELPA algorithm, to determine the optimal number of aggregation 
layers k. By evaluating inter-community and intra-community distances, this strategy ensures a fine-tuned 
aggregation process, achieving a balance between compact intra-community structures and well-separated 
inter-community relationships. This mechanism enhances the model’s ability to handle diverse and complex 
network structures.
A similarity preservation module further refines the node representations by maintaining local similarity and 
global dissimilarity in the smoothed features. This module ensures that critical local features are retained, which 
complements the global structural insights provided by the aggregation process. Together with the DPCELPA 
algorithm, this hybrid design results in a robust and efficient framework for community detection.
Comprehensive experiments across four real-world datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, and ogbn-arxiv) 
demonstrate that GATFELPA consistently outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of accuracy, NMI, 
ARI, and F1-score. Ablation studies underscore the essential contributions of the aggregation and similarity 
preservation modules, while sensitivity analyses validate the effectiveness of the adaptive strategy for optimizing 
k. Computational complexity comparisons further highlight GATFELPA ’s scalability and efficiency, particularly 
on large-scale datasets such as ogbn-arxiv.
In conclusion, GATFELPA represents a significant advancement in community detection, offering a scalable, 
robust, and accurate solution for the analysis of complex graph networks. Its ability to integrate local and global 
graph information while maintaining computational efficiency establishes it as a valuable tool for both academic 
research and practical applications in graph-based data analysis. Future research could explore extending the 
model to dynamic or heterogeneous graphs, further enhancing its versatility in addressing real-world challenges.
