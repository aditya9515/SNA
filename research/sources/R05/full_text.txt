Graph embedding based label 
propagation for community 
detection in social networks
Shyam Sundar Meena1, Prakash Chandra Sharma2, Yadvendra Pratap Singh3 &  
Mukund Pratap Singh4
Community structures are common features of many real-world networks, and community detection 
is necessary to understand how these networks are organized. Various approaches have been devised 
for community detection, with each providing varying degrees of both accuracy and structural 
understanding. One of them, the Label Propagation Algorithm, is so common because it is simple and 
computationally cheap. Nevertheless, it does not usually reach great modularity and yields inaccurate 
community counts and structures in real-world networks. This is mostly due to its naive criteria of 
selecting the neighbor nodes when it comes to label propagation. To tackle the issue, we developed an 
adjusted algorithm, which we call Embedding-based Label Propagation (ELP), a hybrid between LPA 
and node embedding that allows us to combine both local connectivity and global structural data. ELP 
update step takes into consideration not only the local neighborhood, as in conventional LPA, but also 
embedding-based similarities to inform more productive neighbor selection. We tested ELP on popular 
benchmark datasets such as Karate Club, Dolphins, Football, Polbooks, and LFR synthetic networks 
and compared its results with LPA and other well-established algorithms. The empirical findings show 
that ELP can always perform better in modularity, NMI and NF1 scores, but it is also scalable to large 
and complex networks. These results can be used to identify ELP as an effective and powerful method 
of community-finding in real and artificial-world scenarios.
Keywords Label propagation algorithm, Social network, Community detection, Graph embedding, 
Modularity
In the real world, data of various domains follow complex network structures that can be modelled mathematically 
as a graphical structure, where nodes and links between them represent objects and relationships. Example of 
such networks are social networks1, protein-protein networks2, and citation networks3. One of the characteristics 
of complex networks is the community structure4–6 Such structures are characterized by a high degree of inter 
connectivity within community while having inter few interrelationships with nodes outside of the community. 
The community in literature also referred to as clusters or modules 7,8. All in all, knowledge of community 
structures enhances our understanding of social and other networks. Knowing community structure can assist 
academics, scientists, and even ordinary people see connectivity patterns and engagements in these networks.
Several techniques have already been presented in the scientific literature to address group detecting issues. 
One important algorithm is the Label Propagation Algorithm (LPA). LPA is widely used since it requires 
minimal execution duration and is easy. But it falls short in precision within regards to modularity, and the total 
amount of communities discovered per count, and the organization of communities in actual networks. This 
problem arises because neighboring node picking rules in the label propagation mechanism are poorly designed. 
Several advanced algorithms have been suggested to address current LPA9 issues, including10–14. The most recent 
algorithm is LBLD (Local Balanced Label Diffusion)10. The LBLD algorithm tries to make forming communities 
faster by looking at both inside and outside views. However, it has some issues. First, its way of updating labels 
only focuses on important nodes. This means it attracts too many other nodes to those communities, which can 
spoil the quality of the results. Second, it starts by grouping a central node, its closest neighbor, and its shared 
neighbors.

Our proposed new approach is em-bedding-based label propagation (ELP) in order to discover communities 
in complex networks correctly. The proposed algorithm rearranges the LPA with the nodes embeddings, so that 
it can draw both global- and local information intertwined in a more optimal way, especially the big and complex 
networks. The ELP algorithm update step looks at the local neighborhood (like the traditional LPA) and the 
similarities between the node embeddings for neighbor node selection.

Related work
In order to enhance the efficacy of the LPA 9 in community discovery activities, certain academicians have 
suggested modifications. The suggested approaches are developed to solve unique problems that existed in the 
initial LPA, including inefficiency, unpredictability and instability.
Zhang et al. 15 developed a method for detecting communities within networks and devised a way of 
measuring a node’s importance. This approach looks at two things: the k-shell value and the node’s number of 
connections. They also developed something called label influence, which improves the effectiveness of label 
distribution and determines how a label impacts nearby nodes. Using this strategy, they chose the label that 
made the most difference rather than randomly selecting one when there were numerous options. This made the 
outcomes more consistent.
The article16 talks about a three-step process focusing on ranking nodes. This method looks at both local and 
big-picture network features. First, nodes are ranked by how important they are. They use a special index that 
checks how well a node connects with nearby nodes and its place in the larger network. In the second step, they 
focus on the top nodes, which they call seed nodes. Then in the last step, they use a local growth strategy. This 
means they keep adding nearby nodes to form a tight-knit community based on connections and closeness.
Kong and colleagues designed a technique that considers both the node’s relevance and the label’s impact17. 
Furthermore, they developed a unique tightening mechanism that may also be utilised for label propagation. An 
approach was devised by Yue et al.18 to amend the labels of nodes to enhance the procedure’s efficiency. Using the 
K-shell methodology, Deng et al.19 enhanced the node importance model. Zarezade and his colleagues proposed 
a methodology for ascertaining node labels in their research. This methodology entailed a combination of 
heterogeneous node scoring and where the border nodes and labels would be updated simultaneously20.
An approach known as Skip-LPA was presented by Thakare and colleagues in their 2016 publication 21. Due 
to the fact that the technique only initialised a limited portion of the nodes responsible for label propagation, 
the total number of iterations performed by the algorithm was drastically decreased. The initial creators of the 
CDIC algorithm were Yuan and his collaborators 22. The techniques of the algorithm provided many ways to 
solve the problem, one of them being the impact of the core nodes on the diffusion of labels given to the nodes. 
Also, the algorithm merged nodes by using the interests of the community of a given set of nodes. This made 
the technique feasible. Li et al. 23 and Lin et al. 24 were both concerned with stabilising their algorithmic results, 
the former through the use of the modularity function, and the latter with community core initialisation. It was 
these very issues that proved the motivation for dealing with instability.
Zhao et al.25 did extensive community detection in order to reduce the temporal complexity of the technique. 
This was achieved through graph compression and label propagation. It was done to enhance the overall 
effectiveness of the algorithm.In their study, Roghani et al. 10 proposed a technique of label propagation which 
uses local similarity metric to weigh the importance of different nodes. The technique of Bouyer et al. devote to 
the growth of the low degree node sclustering community14. Zhang et al.26 and Zhai et al.27 described techniques 
with with and without core nodes and sequential label propagation carried out by the core nodes.
The paper 28 discusses the problem of community detection in the incomplete networks, in which the presence 
of missing edges due to privacy, security, or other limitations results in poor performance of both overall and 
deep learning-based approaches. Existing methods, including graph partitioning, modularity optimization, 
and link prediction, are inappropriate in these contexts- link prediction, though can be useful at recovering 
edges, is not always useful at improving community detection, and many deep models require node attributes 
or labels that are not available in real-world networks. The authors suggest the Deep Core Node Information 
Embedding (DCNIE) algorithm that combines edge augmentation to maintain topology, k-core decomposition 
to obtain dense sub-network information, a deep unsupervised embedding model with a parametrization layer 
to learn features efficiently, and Gaussian mixture clustering to partition them accurately in order to overcome 
these limitations. In contrast to previous efforts that make much use of edge recovery, DCNIE focuses more 
on core node data to generate robust and stable community detection. Experimental validation on eleven real 
world datasets with eight evaluation measures, shows that DCNIE is always superior to eleven state-of-the-art 
algorithms in accuracy and efficiency, providing a more robust community detention in network with missing 
edges.
Shen, Cencheng et al. 29 suggests a Principal Community Encoder Embedding (P-GEE) algorithm that 
jointly identifies principal communities and produces vertex embeddings by prioritizing the significance of 
a community using a new sample community score. The dimensionality reduction and enhanced interpret-
ability and computational efficiency of the method, by only retaining the dimensions of the largest communities, 
preserves conditional density under the random Bernoulli graph model to guarantee Bayes-optimal classification 
of vertices. The reason is supported by a large body of theoretical study and evidence of the usefulness of the 
approach, and simulations and real-world experiments confirm the benefits of the method in incorporating 
visualization, ground-truth community detection, classification accuracy, and resistance to label noise and 
redundant communities. P-GEE is less opaque, more scalable, and robust, relative to current graph embedding 
models, such as node2vec, spectral, and graph neural networks, due to a non-iterative architecture and its lack of

Proposed work
The goal of community detection is to find dense sub-clusters or modules within a network. A network can be 
represented as a graph 30. In a graph, G(V, E), with V  vertices and E edges, community detection partition 
G(V, E) into i clusters, where C = {c1,c 2,c 3,...,c i} is a collection of communities, where each ci ⊆ V (G) 
represents a set of nodes referred to as a community.
Community detection approach

Distance Calculation with Embeddings, Label Propagation with Embeddings, and Community Detection. The 
descriptions of these tasks are given as follows:

 4. Community Detection: After the stabilization of the propagation process, the last labels are obtained to 
detect communities.
Based on the above approach, a algorithm  1 is developed for the community detection and it is named as a 
Embedding based Label Propagation (ELP) Algorithm . ELP algorithm is an attempt to merge the label 
propagation approach with the node embedding-based information to identify communities of the graph. It 
begins by loading a graph, G(V, E) which consists of nodes (V ) and edges (E).
Once loaded, the algorithm uses the Node2V ec algorithm to generate a node embedding. These results 
represent an embedding of the nodes in terms of random walks and skip-gram model. Setting such as embeddings 
size (64), walk length (30), walks (200) and threads (4) can be modified to make the warm up of embeddings 
faster.
Next, the algorithm calculates cosine similarity to create a similarity matrix. This matrix measures how 
close nodes are to each other. Each node starts with its own label, usually its index number. The Hybrid Label 
Propagation process uses the similarity matrix to weigh how much influence nearby nodes have. In each step, 
nodes are selected randomly.They use labels of their neighbors, as well as the similarity values. The latest labels 
are made using the most powerful votes. This is carried out until the process converges or a maximum number 
of 100 iterations is attained.
Upon completion of this procedure, this assigns community identities to all the vertices, indicating their 
respective group affiliations. The outcomes can be depicted through colouring nodes according to the associated 
groups, facilitating the observation of the organization of the graph. This combination of techniques integrates 
label propagation with node embeddings. It achieves an effective equilibrium across velocity as well as precision, 
rendering it advantageous for domains such as social networking sites, biological ones, and systems that offer 
recommendations.
Time complexity analysis
The ELP algorithm improves the classical LPA on a bottom-up basis. To obtain node embeddings, ELP handles 
a tensor decomposition problem after modeling the network as a tensor and projecting the nodes into a lower-
dimensional embedding space. ELP and random walks are used to solve problems of instability, randomness, 
and resolution in classic LPA. Classical LPA runs in O (V + E) time complexity and depends only on the network 
topology. However, it oscillates in the labeling assigned to nodes and partitions the network in an unstable 
manner. ELP , on the other hand, employs node2vec and label propagation combined with embeddings to capture 
more structural and feature-based relations to achieve more stability and better detection quality.
Unlike modularity-based algorithms such as Louvain, which, like ELP , runs in O(VlogV) time, ELP shows the 
ability to detect more granular community structures, something modularity-based methods generally overlook 
because of the resolution limit. Infomap is better than ELP in that it can identify hierarchical communities 
through random walks, but it can struggle with dense graphs and produce communities that are irregularly 
shaped. ELP , on the other hand, improves the consistency of partitions formed as a result of random walk 
convergence through the embedding similarity approach. From a computation point of view, ELP adds an extra 
step during the generation of embeddings. Take, for instance, the semi-definite approximation of epigraphs. Its 
complexity is O(V 2 d), where V is the number of nodes and d is the embedding dimension. Each node is processed 
sequentially during the random walks for the node2vec embedding generation and matrix construction, yielding 
an overall embedding generation and matrix construction complexity that can be expressed as a linear function 
of V and E. While there is some overhead as compared to the linear time complexity associated with LPA, the 
increase in modularity, stability, and robustness makes the overhead worthwhile.
In conclusion, while algorithms like LPA, Louvain, Leiden, and Infomap are computationally efficient, their 
lack of stability, accuracy, and insensitivity to resolution of communities result in poor performance on complex 
networks. Community detection algorithms that utilize embeddings, like ELP , strike an ideal compromise 
between scalability and quality, which is why it is well suited for social, biological, and citation networks where 
relational semantics are important. Complex as it may be relative to traditional LPA, it is ELP’ s greater within-
cluster density stability, more within-cluster density interpretability, and enhanced quality of the community 
overall that elevates it as more dependable when precise community identification is the main goal.

Result and discussion
Multiple real-world and artificially generated networks of different sizes were used for experiments. The objective 
of the experiments was to compare the quality and accuracy of the alternative methods of community detection 
that were evaluated with the aid of various measures. All the experiments were performed in Google Colab. 
For the experimental analysis, LPA 9 implementation provided by NetworkX library32, PNLP4, LBLD10, PSN33, 
Leiden34, Louvain35 and Infomap36 were used.
To evaluate the count of communities detected with the original LPA and with Recent LPA Variants and ELP , 
dimensions such as detected communities, modularity, NMI, and NF1-Score were calculated for each of the 
networks, and then compared across all networks.

 4. Normalized F1(NF1) score  Recall is described as the ratio of true positive vertices classified by the algo -

contrary, precision is the ratio of true positive vertices to the total number of vertices that an algorithm has 
classified as belonging to that community, reflecting the thoroughness of the estimator. A survey which aims 
to assess a trade-off of precision and recall would compute the F1 score, whch is the derived as the harmonic 
mean of the two. This score is representative of a community’s relative measure of balance between the total 
and true number of detected community constituents.

Experiments on real benchmark network
In the experiment, the real-benchmark networks highly used in the domain of social networks analysis were 
selected. In addition, each of these networks is offered at no cost with the intended use of conducting experiments. 
In the present investigation, four distinct networks are selected, such as the Zachary karate network(ZKN) 37, 
Dolphins network38, Pol-books network39 and Political blog network (PBN)40. The detail description is given as 
follows:
 1. Zachary Karate Network: The network has 34 members, denoted by 34 nodes in graph representation, and 
78 edges model the relationships between members. Wayne Zachary collected the data and modeled it in the 
1970s as an undirected graph. Due to some dispute between two tutors at a karate club, two sections emerged.
 2. Polbooks Network: It is a compilation of political writings aligned to the United States which came out in 
the year 2004. It can be procured from the online retail store Amazon.com. The interconnections among the 
volumes portray that the same consumers tend to get multiple copies of the same titles.
 3. Dolphins Network: The dolphins’ relationship constitutes a network of 159 edges and 62 vertices. 62 distinct 
dolphin species and interrelations of kinship were used to construct this network. Lusseau et al. 38 created a 
graph pertaining to this member’s relations without direction.
 4. Political Blogs Network: It is a structured, interlinked web. The interlinks demonstrate the connections be-
tween blogs. The network consists of two components: liberal and conservative. The entire blogsite network 
contains 1490 nodes and 19090 links.

In addition to such performance benefits, there are the added benefits of ELP . The embedding-based design is 
more stable in the detecting of the community, firstly, because the method is less sensitive to random initialization 
and stochastic dynamics that tend to restrict the traditional LPA methods. Second, the reported modularity 
benefits imply that ELP can identify more coherent and clean communities and this is especially relevant to 
systems that need to trust the community structure to be well-defined, like the analysis of biological networks or 
recommendation systems. Lastly, the embedded-based structure of ELP also makes it very appropriate to large, 
and dynamically changing networks where scalability and adaptability are paramount. With network systems 
becoming increasingly more complex and dynamic, the capacity of ELP to stay stable, attain modular partitions, 
and scale is an important indicator to note that ELP is capable of being a powerful and highly adaptable 
instrument in the real-world community detection process.

Conclusion
The study proposes a novel algorithm called Embedding-based Label Propagation which optimally improves 
community. ELP uses local neighborhood and global structure data to overcome the limitations of traditional 
LPA and produces more prominent, stable, and coherent communities across different types of networks. 
Performance evaluation on synthetic and real-world benchmark data shows ELP improves modularity, accuracy 
and robustness compared to traditional algorithms but retains the scalability and simplicity of LPA. ELP retains 
the same low complexity as label propagation while providing more certainty on large complex networks due to 
reduced randomness.
We recognize that embedding computing nodes comes with extra overhead, thereby making ELP slower than 
the original LPA. The next line of work to be undertaken in future is to streamline embedding computation and 
to consider parallel or distributed methods of processing to drive scaling and efficiency even higher. The exciting 
future potential would be to scale ELP to dynamic and overlapping community detection which will increase its 
applicability to real-world networks that change through time and have multiplexing structures.
